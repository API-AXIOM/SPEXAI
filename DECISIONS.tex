\section*{2026-09-15: DEM log-$T$ switch (bias campaign)}

\paragraph{Parametrisation.} \emph{Chosen:} Gaussian in $\log_{10}T$
(SPEX \texttt{gdem}) for the bias campaign. \emph{Tried:} Gaussian in linear
$T$ (thesis parametrisation). \emph{Why:} on the log-spaced grid a log-$T$
width has constant resolution; the linear-$T$ width spanned 0.6--20 cells across
one design and produced the single $>1\sigma$ outlier (1.642) as a resolution
artefact.

\paragraph{Temperature design range.} \emph{Chosen:} $0.7$--$15\kev$ for both
the DEM centre and single-$T$ $kT$, drawn uniform in $\log_{10}T$.
\emph{Tried/considered:} $1.5$--$8\kev$ (previous, uncited), $1$--$12\kev$,
$0.5$--$15\kev$. \emph{Why:} groups through the most massive clusters.
$0.5\kev$ rejected: below the PCHIP truth's safe floor ($0.7\kev$; Ar diverges
at $0.5\kev$), below the emulator floor ($0.5013\kev$), and where the cache step
defects cluster (Na, Fe, Ni, Cu, Co at $0.52$--$0.67\kev$).

\paragraph{Width range.} \emph{Chosen:} $\sigma_x = 0.056$--$0.4$\,dex, no cap
tied to the centre. \emph{Tried:} a centre-dependent cap requiring $\ge98\%$ of
the DEM on the grid. \emph{Why rejected:} SPEX does not restrict \texttt{gdem}
to fully contained Gaussians, and truth and emulator share the same
$0.7$--$19.94\kev$ grid, so a truncated DEM is truncated identically on both
sides and $b_{\rm sys}$ remains a fair comparison. The floor is a resolution
limit ($\approx2.7$ cells of the 70-node grid). The contained fraction is
recorded per truth point for interpretation instead.

\paragraph{Fit bounds and steps.} \emph{Chosen:} $\log T$ centre and $\log kT$:
design range $\pm0.1$\,dex; $\sigma_x$: $0.045$--$0.5$\,dex; finite-difference
step $5\times10^{-4}$\,dex for both DEM parameters. \emph{Why:} bounds are not
physics; they only need never to bind at a truth point and to stay inside the
emulator's trained range. The width's lower bound sits just below the
resolution floor. Single-$T$ $kT$ keeps its $5\times10^{-3}\kev$ step
(unchanged; to revisit if the step check flags it at $0.7\kev$).

\paragraph{Campaign fiducial.} \emph{Chosen:} $\log_{10}T = 0.591$,
$\sigma_x = 0.111$\,dex, converted from $3.9\pm1.0\kev$ at the centre
($\sigma_x = \sigma_T/(T\ln 10)$).

\paragraph{Perseus showcase.} \emph{Chosen:} stays linear $T$. \emph{Why:} the
XRISM Perseus enrichment analysis (arXiv:2606.17141) fits XSPEC
\texttt{bvvgadem}, whose $\sigma_T$ is in keV; the showcase should match the
real analysis. Grid and fiducial deferred.

\paragraph{Frozen scripts.} \emph{Chosen:} hot\_floor scripts, \texttt{dump\_truth},
\texttt{bake\_off} and the cache-step diagnostics are not modified; their
divergence from the campaign is recorded in \texttt{NOTES.md}. \emph{Why:} not
needed for P6/P7, and their produced results must remain reproducible.
Consequence: \texttt{campaign.gaussian\_dem} and \texttt{campaign.build\_params}
keep the linear-$T$ path; the campaign uses new \texttt{gaussian\_logT\_dem}.

\paragraph{Finite-difference step (2026-09-16).} \emph{Chosen:} one step in
dex, $5\times10^{-4}$, for every temperature axis; $kT$ takes it converted at
the point, $kT\ln 10\,\times 5\times10^{-4}$, replacing the inherited absolute
$5\times10^{-3}\kev$. \emph{Why:} the step check at the widened range showed the
DEM parameters comfortably in the valley between truncation ($\propto h^2$) and
float32 round-off ($\propto1/h$) --- halving the step moved the Fisher-weighted
Jacobian column by $7.7\times10^{-5}$ to $8.3\times10^{-4}$ --- while single-$T$
$kT$ moved by $6.0\times10^{-2}$ at $0.75\kev$ against $3.1\times10^{-3}$ at
$14\kev$. An absolute step is $0.67\%$ of $kT$ at the cold end and $0.036\%$ at
the hot end, and above $1.9\kev$ a cold plasma contributes only its exponential
tail, so the curvature there is extreme; the implied Jacobian bias at the old
step is ${\sim}8\%$. \texttt{campaign.build\_params} keeps $5\times10^{-3}\kev$
(frozen hot\_floor path, fiducial $3.9\kev$, where it is $0.13\%$ of $kT$).

\paragraph{Outcome of that change (2026-09-16), and a correction.} The rerun
refuted the reasoning above. With the relative step, single-$T$ $kT$ at
$0.75\kev$ moved from $6.8\times10^{-2}$ to $3.8\times10^{-2}$ and at $14\kev$
from $3.1\times10^{-3}$ to $6.6\times10^{-3}$; the DEM values were unchanged.
Shrinking the step $5.8\times$ at the cold end should have reduced the
difference ${\sim}34\times$ under an $h^2$ truncation law and reduced it
$1.8\times$, and neither end follows the round-off law either. Step size is
therefore not what controls the cold-end number, and the ${\sim}8\%$ Jacobian
bias inferred above does not follow from the data. \emph{The relative step is
retained} on the grounds of units---a single step in dex serves $0.7$--$15\kev$
where an absolute keV step cannot---not on that measurement.

What remains unexplained is confined to the cold points. Above $1.9\kev$ a
$0.75\kev$ plasma leaves nearly every channel with $\mu\simeq0$, and both the
check and \texttt{linear\_bias\_fisher} weight channels by ${\sim}1/\mu$, so
empty channels can dominate on float32 noise alone. Whether $J$ is genuinely
unreliable below ${\sim}1.5\kev$, the check misreports it, or the band simply
carries no information there is not resolved; the diagnostic that separates
them (three step sizes, a signal-restricted metric, and a count of empty
channels) is specified but was not run. This affects only the coldest Tier~B
points, where $\mathrm{cond}(F)$ is reported per point; $k$ is insulated,
because the Gauss--Newton fixed point is set by the autograd score and $F$ only
preconditions the path.

\paragraph{Forward-mode AD instead of a step: probed, rejected for now.}
\emph{Tried:} replacing the central-difference Jacobian with
\texttt{torch.func.jvp}. Forward mode is the right shape for this object (one
pass per parameter, versus one per channel for reverse mode), it would be about
twice as cheap as the $2n+1$ stencil, and it has no step at all.
\emph{Result:} it fails on both paths --- \texttt{Sparse CSR tensors do not have
strides} in the response fold (DEM), and \texttt{You must implement the jvp
function for custom autograd.Function} from the trunk's gradient checkpointing
(single-$T$). An abundance control agreed to $5.8\times10^{-4}$ by finite
differences in both modes, so the probe itself was sound.
\emph{Why not fixed now:} both are fixable --- the fold is linear, so forward
mode need only run as far as \texttt{flux} and the same map applies to the
tangent, and checkpointing is redundant under forward mode --- but it is a
hot-path restructure with an uncertain interaction with \texttt{grad\_enabled},
to fix one parameter at one end of the design that the relative step already
fixes. Recorded as future work; probe kept at
\texttt{scripts/inference/probe\_jacobian\_ad.py}.

\section*{2026-09-16: Gauss--Newton bugs (D1--D3)}

\paragraph{D1, vacuous convergence: FIXED.} \emph{Chosen:} \texttt{converged}
now requires \texttt{resolved.any()}, and the record carries
\texttt{n\_resolved}. \emph{Why:} \texttt{frac} is zeroed wherever a
parameter's bias is unresolved, so a point that resolved nothing scored
$\max(\texttt{frac})=0$ and reported converged---``the fit learned nothing''
became ``the fit converged''. All six converged DEM points of the 2026-09-10
run were that case, each clamp-saturated at $\texttt{drift\_sigma}=20.000$, and
their $k$ was read as a result. The criterion was extracted from
\texttt{run\_point} into \texttt{p6\_sweep.convergence\_verdict} so it could be
tested at all; three tests added.

\paragraph{D2, Gauss--Newton does not descend: HELD.} $-\log L$ rises
monotonically while the Newton decrement falls. \emph{Deferred by D.H.} until
the log-$T$ switch and D1 have been through a real screen, on the grounds that
the other fixes may change the picture. The proposed fix, if it is still needed,
is to accept a step only on decrease and otherwise halve along the same Newton
direction---affine invariant, and not the strong-Wolfe search that broke
L-BFGS.

\paragraph{D3, unreachable spread criterion: more iterations.} \emph{Chosen:}
raise the iteration budget (\texttt{--gn\_iter} $8\to20$), plus a start-up
check that warns when $\texttt{gn\_iter}\times\texttt{gn\_max\_step}$ is smaller
than the point's actual start spread. \emph{Why:} the noiseless starts scatter
by ${\sim}|b_{\rm sys}|$ per component---$60$--$280\sigma$ between the most
distant pair---while the trust region caps each iteration at $20\sigma$, so
closing the gap needs $\ge14$ iterations before convergence can begin and the
budget was $8$--$12$. The criterion therefore failed by construction. This is
affordable only now: at ${\sim}12$\,s per iteration $20$ iterations is
${\sim}4$ minutes per point, where at $980$\,s it was $3.3$ hours.
\emph{Rejected:} a larger step clamp (trades a known cost for overshoot risk
into a region where the dropped residual term is not small) and tighter starts
via \texttt{--start\_bsys\_scale} (closer starts agree more easily, so the
multi-start certificate would certify less). Note the budget only helps if the
iteration descends, i.e.\ D2; both should be re-read against the new screen.

\paragraph{TwoGaussianDEM.} \emph{Chosen:} no longer renormalised, matching
\texttt{ParametricDEM}. \emph{Why:} renormalising silently redistributes an
off-grid component's emission measure onto the grid.

\section*{2026-09-17: the DEM screen, and what $k$ can be read from}

\paragraph{D2, Gauss--Newton does not descend: CLOSED, no fix needed.}
\emph{Observed:} across all 30 points and 120 fits of the log-$T$ screen,
$-\log L$ falls monotonically --- point~0 runs $+1.22\times10^3$ to $-112.6$ in
two iterations --- and $\max|s\sigma|$ falls with it, $570\to0.54$. The
ascent-direction failure recorded on 2026-09-16 does not reproduce under the
log-$T$ parametrisation. \emph{Why it was right to hold:} D.H.\ deferred the
line-search fix pending a real screen, precisely on the grounds that the other
changes might change the picture, and they did. No accept-on-decrease logic was
added.

\paragraph{D3, unreachable spread criterion: retired in practice.}
\emph{Observed:} the start-to-start spread collapsed from $60$--$280\sigma$
under linear $T$ to $0.003$--$0.039\sigma$, and the start-up warning added on
2026-09-16 never fired at any point. Convergence now takes $2$--$7$ iterations
(mode $2$--$3$); all 120 fits exit on the Newton decrement, none stalled and
none exhausted the budget. \emph{Consequence:} the $8\to20$ iteration budget is
kept but is no longer binding.

\paragraph{Fast convergence is not a bug.} \emph{Investigated} because two- to
four-iteration convergence looked too good to be true. Fisher scoring on a
Poisson likelihood that is near-quadratic at $10^9$ counts, started
${\sim}b_{\rm sys}$ away, should converge quadratically; the descent of
$-\log L$, the collapse of the score, and the agreement of eight independent
starts to $<0.04\sigma$ all confirm it is genuine. \emph{Recorded} so the
question is not reopened.

\paragraph{\texttt{se\_delta} is not an error bar in noiseless mode.}
\emph{Found:} the noiseless redesign made the $K$ rows independent \emph{starts}
on one objective rather than one start on $K$ noisy objectives, so their scatter
measures optimiser repeatability (${\sim}1.5\times10^{-5}\sigma$), not
statistical uncertainty. Every threshold built on it is therefore vacuous:
\texttt{measurable} $=|b_{\rm sys}|>3\,\texttt{se\_d}$ holds for all $390$
point-parameters at a median margin of ${\sim}1.4\times10^{4}$, and
\texttt{resolved} --- the guard D1 restored on 2026-09-16 --- holds for $13/13$
parameters at every point, so \texttt{resolved.any()} can never fire.
\emph{Consequence:} D1's fix is correct for the failure it was built from and
inert against this one. \emph{Chosen:} $k$ is read only above an absolute floor
on its denominator, $|b_{\rm sys}|\ge0.5\sigma$, which is already the convention
documented for the single-$T$ sweep in the methodology note; the
\texttt{se\_delta}-based detectability rule is not to be used in noiseless mode.
\emph{Not yet implemented in code} --- \texttt{p6\_sweep} still writes
\texttt{measurable} and \texttt{converged} on the old rule, and this analysis
was done downstream of the jsonl.

\paragraph{Convergence judged relative to the bias: same root cause.}
\emph{Found:} \texttt{drift\_frac\_of\_bias} divides the fit's residual movement
by the bias, so a point whose bias is negligible can never converge. Ten of 30
points are flagged \texttt{NOT CONVERGED} while their absolute drift,
$0.010$--$0.135\sigma$, is in line with the converged ones; point~17 reports
$62\times$ the bias on a drift of $0.022\sigma$ because its smallest resolved
bias is $1.2\times10^{-4}\sigma$. \emph{Chosen:} judge drift against
$\max(\text{bias},\text{floor})$, so that ``nothing to measure'' reports as
converged rather than failed. \emph{Also not yet implemented.}

\paragraph{The $0.5\sigma$ cut, and the error bar on $k$.} \emph{Chosen:}
$|b_{\rm sys}|\ge0.5\sigma$ rather than $1\sigma$, by D.H., to keep more of the
sample; $305/390$ point-parameters survive. \emph{Uncertainty model:}
$\mathrm{unc}_k=\max(\texttt{drift\_sigma},\texttt{start\_spread\_sigma})/
(|b_{\rm sys}|/\sigma)$, the fit's own convergence floor propagated through the
ratio. \emph{Why it is incomplete, and it matters:} it accounts only for the
numerator. $b_{\rm sys}$ carries its own error from the finite-difference
Jacobian and the Fisher solve, which is not propagated, and the symptom is
visible --- the most significant deviations are the \emph{smallest} ones at the
largest denominators (point~5 Ar, $k=0.97$, flagged at $20\,\mathrm{unc}_k$). A
$5$--$10\%$ error on $b_{\rm sys}$ alone would account for the observed scatter.
\emph{Consequence:} the $\pm0.11$ spread in $k$ is reported as unattributed ---
either $b_{\rm sys}$'s own error bar (random, averages away over a sweep) or
genuine second-order curvature (structured, and would need carrying per
parameter). The discriminator, not run, is whether $|k-1|$ shrinks as
$|b_{\rm sys}|/\sigma$ grows, as it does in the single-$T$ sweep.

\paragraph{Reporting $k$ as a single ratio: kept, with the floor.}
\emph{Rejected:} replacing the per-point ratio with a regression slope of
$\delta/\sigma$ on $b_{\rm sys}/\sigma$. It handles small denominators correctly
by construction (short lever arms get little leverage, no threshold to tune) and
its intercept would expose the convergence floor, but it presumes the correction
is one global constant, which is the wrong shape for a question about how
offsets vary \emph{across} parameter space, and a single slope would average
straight through any curvature. \emph{Chosen:} report $k$ per point-parameter
above the floor, which answers the question directly and shows no anomaly worth
modelling. \emph{Result:} $k=0.99\pm0.11$, every per-parameter median in
$0.94$--$1.02$, slightly below unity, i.e.\ the screen mildly over-predicts the
DEM bias.

\section*{2026-09-18: the widened single-$T$ screen, and D4 implemented}

\paragraph{D2 REOPENED.} \emph{Correction to 2026-09-17,} which closed D2 on
the DEM screen's evidence. The widened single-$T$ screen refutes that closure:
three of 30 points exit through the stall detector while pinned at the
$20\sigma$ trust-region boundary, and at two of them $-\log L$ is \emph{rising}
at exit (point 0 runs $-2.149\times10^4 \to -2.141\times10^4 \to
-2.119\times10^4$ over its last three iterations). Gauss--Newton does move
uphill, but only in the clamp-saturated regime, which the DEM screen never
entered --- so the earlier closure was premature rather than wrong on its own
data. \emph{Why it was right to hold D2 originally:} D.H.\ declined to accept a
fix before a real screen had exercised it, and two screens were needed.

\paragraph{Mechanism of the three failures: the clamp is isotropic.}
\emph{Found:} the trust region rescales the \emph{entire} step vector by
whatever its worst single component demands (\texttt{over = |d/sig|.max() /
max\_step\_sigma}, then \texttt{d /= over}), so one runaway direction throttles
every other direction by the same factor. Direction-dependence within one fit
is the signature: at point 0 the $\sigma_v$ direction converged completely (the
across-start scatter fell to $1.7\%$ of its initial value) while S never moved
at all ($99.4\%$), in the same eight starts; at point 18 several
thick-denominator directions ended \emph{further} apart than they began.
\emph{Chosen:} record the mechanism, fix nothing now. \emph{Why:} $k$
replicates across three independent samples, the three points are 10\% of one
of them, and changing the optimiser would mean the DEM and single-$T$ screens
were produced by different code. \emph{Rejected for now:} per-component
clamping, and accepting a step only on decrease. Both remain the right fixes
whenever this stage is rerun.

\paragraph{The failures are not regional.} \emph{Checked} before excluding
them, because excluding a difficult corner would flatter the result. Their
percentiles within the 30-point design are $3/33/70$ in $kT$, $47/17/60$ in
$\sigma_v$, $27/30/90$ in $n_{\rm H}$ and $43/3/63$ in $\mathrm{cond}(F)$.
\emph{Two hypotheses refuted:} ``they had furthest to travel'' (point 18 has
the third-\emph{smallest} predicted bias in the design, while points 10 and 16
carry the two largest and converge cleanly) and ``they are badly conditioned''
(point 1 has the second-\emph{lowest} $\mathrm{cond}(F)$ of all 30).

\paragraph{D4 implemented: absolute floors on both thresholds.}
\emph{Chosen:} \texttt{measurable\_mask} requires
$|b_{\rm sys}| > \max(3\,\texttt{se\_d},\ 0.5\,\sigma)$, and
\texttt{convergence\_verdict} judges drift and start spread against
$\max(\text{bias},\ 1\,\sigma)$ at the same $10\%$ convention. \emph{Why:}
every threshold in the estimator divided by a quantity that vanishes, which
failed at both ends --- a point with no measurable bias could never converge,
and a point that resolved nothing converged vacuously. \emph{Consequence:} the
floor subsumes the \texttt{resolved.any()} precondition added for D1, which is
therefore dropped from the criterion; \texttt{n\_resolved} is kept as a
diagnostic, and the \texttt{np.where(resolved, ..., 0.0)} masks --- the
mechanism of the vacuous score --- are gone. \emph{Verified:} 296 tests pass
(293 + 3 new), and on the stored single-$T$ run the new rule reports 3 points
not converged where the old reported 9, with no indeterminate cases, and
tightens \texttt{measurable} from $347/360$ to $301/360$. \emph{Kept
deliberately:} the two \texttt{*\_frac\_of\_bias} key names, although their
denominator is now floored, because \texttt{p6\_trends} and
\texttt{p6\_check\_outliers} consume them to exclude points and the floor makes
those exclusions strictly better.

\paragraph{Exclusion rule for a sweep: absolute, not relative.}
\emph{Chosen:} drop points whose $\max(\text{drift},\text{spread})$ exceeds
$1\sigma$. \emph{Why:} the separation is three orders of magnitude
($\le0.048\sigma$ for 27 points, $20$--$35\sigma$ for three), so no tuning is
involved. \emph{Note:} the stored \texttt{converged} flag is not usable for
this, being the pre-D4 relative one.

\paragraph{Diagnose with geometry, not per-parameter $k$.} \emph{Chosen:}
report $|\delta|/|b_{\rm sys}|$ and $\cos\theta$ between the predicted and
measured offset vectors in $\sigma$ units, over components with
$|b_{\rm sys}|\ge0.5\sigma$. \emph{Why:} $k\neq1$ conflates a scale error (same
direction, wrong length --- exactly what a scalar $k$ is built to express) with
a direction error (which no scalar can repair and which per-parameter $k$
misrepresents). \emph{Result:} 23 of 27 converged points are right in both
length (to $8\%$) and direction (to ${\sim}2^\circ$); the four exceptions are
points 11, 17, 22 and 28, and point 11's error is predominantly of scale
($1.463$ at $\cos\theta=0.970$). Implemented as
\texttt{scripts/inference/p6\_linearisation\_geometry.py}.

\paragraph{Point 11 is the campaign's real worst case, and it is in
proportion.} \emph{Found:} a cleanly converged point (drift $0.026\sigma$, no
clamp saturation, exit on the decrement) with thick denominators and
$k=1.54$ on $kT$ at $|b_{\rm sys}|=12.58\sigma$, giving $k^2\simeq2.4$.
\emph{Why it does not change the campaign's conclusion:} it \emph{replicates}
the 100-point design's independently measured worst case of $2.75$ rather than
exceeding it, so the tail is a stable property of the linearisation; and no
design coordinate marks it out in advance --- its $\mathrm{cond}(F)$ is in fact
the lowest in the design. \emph{Consequence:} P7 carries the distribution of
$k$ per parameter, not a single scalar, and quotes a worst case near
$1.5\times$ in magnitude.


\paragraph{P7 design: 1000 points per flavour, seed 39235.} \emph{Chosen:}
\texttt{--n\_points 1000} for both the single-temperature and the DEM log-$T$
flavour, with \texttt{--seed 39235} (drawn at random 2026-09-18 and recorded
here so the Latin hypercube is reproducible). \emph{Why the seed is recorded
rather than defaulted:} \texttt{bias\_sweep.sample\_points(n, mode, seed)}
generates a fresh hypercube for every $(n,\text{seed})$ pair, so a different
$n$ is a different design and not an extension of an existing one; the seed is
the only handle on the exact point set. \emph{Consequence:} every P7 result
file carries \texttt{n1000\_s39235} in its name, and any reproduction must use
both numbers together.


\paragraph{The bias stage's Jacobian is batched; the serial path is kept as
the default.} \emph{Chosen:} \texttt{bias\_sweep --stage bias} gains
\texttt{--jacobian batched}, which folds \texttt{--point\_chunk} sweep points'
$2n+1$ stencils into one call of the batched forward P6 ran
(\texttt{mle\_reseed.tierb\_forward}) and then applies the identical Fisher
algebra, extracted as \texttt{fisher\_bias.fisher\_from\_jacobian} so the two
paths cannot drift apart. \emph{Why the serial path stays the default:} every
bias result before 2026-09 was produced by it, and a default flip would make
those commands silently un-reproducible; the risk of forgetting the flag is
handled by a printed cost warning above 50 points instead. \emph{Verified:} on
two fresh points per flavour, $b_{\rm sys}$ agrees to $5.7\times10^{-4}\sigma$
(single-$T$) and $5.0\times10^{-4}\sigma$ (DEM), with $\sigma_{\rm ref}$ to
$2.5\times10^{-4}$ relative --- the float32-versus-float64 level, and far below
anything that moves an $N_\star$ (\texttt{check\_jacobian\_parity.py}).
\emph{Found on the way:} the serial DEM path calls
\texttt{JointOperatorModel.predict\_counts\_dem}, which carries none of the DEM
accelerations (trunk table, contract-before-broadening, kinematic grouping) ---
they live only in the batched forward. Laptop CPU cost is $3705$--$5785$\,s per
DEM point serially against $8.7$\,s batched once the table is built, so the
serial DEM flavour alone would be ${\sim}1600$\,h at 1000 points against the
${\sim}73$\,h the P7 agenda assumed. \emph{Consequence:} the port is a
prerequisite for the DEM flavour, not a speed optimisation, and the single-$T$
speedup remains unmeasured on CPU (1.02x, compute-bound) pending the GPU
timing run.
